# AQIL — Final Adversarial Product and System Review
**Status:** Design judgements; not claims of model performance or competitive-market uniqueness.

## Executive conclusion
**Proceed, but do not build an agent swarm or financial-advice product.** Build a credible, reproducible research instrument with excellent human explanations, then prove whether it generates usable conclusions. The research engine is the main differentiator. The consumption experience and accounting tools make it useful daily. Commercialisation is conditional on rights, legal scope, operational controls and a market willing to pay.

## The principal mistaken assumptions and corrections

| Premise | Failure one level down | Failure another level down | Binding correction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 'Never hallucinate' | Language models sometimes fabricate; original sources can also be wrong | A fluent unsupported statement spreads into investment experiments, editorial pages and trust metrics | Publish through deterministic claim verification; fail closed and display unknown; corrections and incident review, not a zero-error guarantee |
| 'Proven institutional methods work' | Published relationships can be fragile, regime-dependent, proprietary or costly to reproduce | An ETF implementation differs from the assets, leverage, shorting and trading assumptions used in original research | Method registry with replication, execution, rights, fit and independent forward validation labels |
| 'More sources = more intelligence' | Syndicated news, repeating narratives and data revision create fake confirmation | Incorrect confidence compounds across causal edges | Source clustering, origin independence, vintage handling, source hierarchy and explicit uncertainty |
| 'Policy announcements are predictive' | Legislation differs from spending/implementation | Foreign retaliation or substitution reverses the presumed beneficiaries | Track policy lifecycle, appropriations, contracts, physical output, reversals, cross-border effects |
| 'A correct macro forecast makes money' | Expected developments may already be priced | Appropriate ETF may be expensive, diluted or dominated by unrelated holdings | Separate event, transmission, valuation, instrument and portfolio result scores |
| '100 paper portfolios = 100 experiments' | Shared prices and correlated exposures create dependence | Strategy selection among hundreds amplifies false discoveries | Group experiments by preregistered hypothesis family, count independent regimes, hold-out and forward champion cohorts |
| 'The system should continually learn' | Reactive weight updates follow short-run noise | Champion methodology overfits and loses its audit trail | Researcher/challenger/champion separation; independent trials; immutable results and version promotion gates |
| 'Aqil can do round-the-clock research cheaply' | AI browsing, fresh data, licensing and rate-limits cost money | Low-quality free providers create false signals or compromised operations | Cheap source ingestion, selective agents, fixed budgets, data-quality gates, visible stopped/stale states |
| 'Broad ETF shortlist is easy' | ISIN/fund/share class/listing confusion; cost and liquidity ambiguity | Incorrect instruments, FX or distributions corrupt ledger and evaluations | Canonical instruments and official issuer verification; separate exposure attractiveness from fund eligibility |
| 'It should be useful to all skills' | A single dense interface alienates novices | Oversimplified copy misleads experts and confuses financial concepts | One versioned research object; Quick Read / Context / Full Methods; comprehension testing with diverse users |
| 'Make a commute reading habit' | Incentive to publish more sensational content | Engagement optimisation pressures financial recommendations and erodes neutrality | Editorial usefulness, correction and comprehension metrics, no forced finance or false freshness |
| '5% saving funds kids' education' | Actual outcomes depend on pay, timing, fees, inflation and volatility | Lower earners may lack liquidity or carry expensive debt | Compound scenarios with ranges, education on affordability and different horizons, no outcome guarantees |
| 'Free for life' | Ambiguous scope, mass sign-ups, multi-account abuse | Subscription viability or terms dispute when services evolve | Audited founding entitlement; clear offer, verification, fair-use and economic capacity analysis before announcement |
| 'Banker-like data in a retail app' | Financial/licence rights and personal data rules differ between private and public use | Early provider choice blocks public release or exposes account information | Provider matrix with separate use rights; RLS from first migration; privacy and legal launch gates |
| 'Transparent peer review' | Spam and confident but erroneous criticism | Published links distribute malicious content; abusive moderation damages trust | Structured evidence challenge, triage, security sanitation, corrections with independent review and audit |

## Product trade-offs we must keep explicit
**Research quality vs speed:** publish later rather than falsely fresh. **Depth vs comprehension:** progressive disclosure, never strip an essential caveat. **Breadth vs coverage:** 30 domains in taxonomy, few verified collectors in first release. **Personalisation vs regulatory perimeter:** a portfolio can show exposure and overlap without instructing a specific person to trade. **Scientific openness vs copyright/licensing:** show inputs and methods without illegally republishing data. **Never reacting vs alerting:** update beliefs when evidence changes; do not encourage impulsive allocation. **Transparency vs security/privacy:** public research traceability and private account isolation are different disclosure duties.

## Architecture failure cascades: require a control at each level
1. **Source reports incorrect figure → machine extracts cleanly → beautifully explained misinformation:** cross-check original release and revisions, record reported status, block material unmatched figures.
2. **Multiple syndicated articles → apparent consensus → inflated forecast probability:** canonical event clusters and independence tags.
3. **Source accessible now but not then → fictional alpha in backtest:** four clock model (`effective`, `published`, `first_seen`, `available_to_strategy`) and point-in-time queries.
4. **Data feed outage → stale values shown as current → false positive signal:** stale states, upstream health, no silent last-known-as-current.
5. **Source content contains instruction injection → agent exfiltration:** untrusted retrieval contexts, tool allowlists, no credential or arbitrary network permissions in research workflows.
6. **LLM cites valid source but misrepresents it → false claim appears verified:** source-span or deterministic data-field entailment check; independent numerical verification and manual approval.
7. **Correct policy decision → failed implementation → wrong investment:** policy lifecycle and actual spending/output checkpoints.
8. **Correct commodity demand forecast → supply surge → miner losses:** scenario supply elasticity, substitution, output/profit bridge.
9. **Correct forecast → expensive entry → ETF underperformance:** valuation/expectation gate and no-action benchmark.
10. **ETF ticker collision → wrong share class / wrong cashflow:** internal `instrument_id`, ISIN, listing, MIC, currency, distribution policy.
11. **Price-only return used for distributing ETF → false comparison:** instrument-aware total-return rules.
12. **Trade date/settlement and FX mismatched → inaccurate wealth analytics:** decimal-based ledger invariants, market calendars, reconciliation fixtures.
13. **Portfolio import retried → duplicate holdings → fabricated wealth:** importer source IDs, idempotency and reconciliation review.
14. **200 virtual portfolios win by chance → promoted 'best' algorithm:** pre-registered families, multiple-test correction and prospective frozen champion.
15. **AI model/prompt upgraded → old hypotheses reinterpreted:** immutable forecast plus pinned method, code, prompt and model versions.
16. **Correction removes old research → cannot audit performance:** amendments and visible errata, never destructive rewrite.
17. **Revenue from ETF referrals → biased editorial ranking:** compensation registry, separation, prominent disclosures and review.
18. **Free lifetime promise → unexpected costs/abuse:** auditable entitlements, offer terms, verification and feasible capacity gate.
19. **Public launch → cross-user portfolio disclosure through view or export:** private schemas/RLS, explicit grants, deny tests on DB views/storage/export endpoints.
20. **Commute UX rewards sensational alerts → investment behaviour degrades:** quality and comprehension metrics, no FOMO triggers.
21. **Offline PWA caches private account responses → shared-device exposure:** no-store financial responses; only public shells/assets offline by default.
22. **Overconfidence despite small validation cohorts → false appearance of expertise:** minimum sample-size labels, calibration plots and 'insufficient evidence' status.
23. **LLM runs unbounded web research → cost explosion:** dedup, quotas, timeouts, human escalation and circuit breakers.
24. **Brand borrowing looks like airline endorsement:** original Aqil identity; navy/gold *inspired by* palette only; separate trademark/trade-dress review.

## Decision register: what is stable vs what still needs proof
**Stable:** name Aqil; people-first progressive disclosure; research crown jewel; published-method foundation; evidence gates; 12/24/36 months; paper laboratory; core diversified ETF philosophy; manual portfolio; no auto trading; private-first multi-tenant-ready PWA; Aptos-first restrained white/navy/gold; design tokens; founding-account benefit conditional on clearly defined published terms.

**Unproven (do not sell as fact):** alpha, forecast skill, public willingness to pay, ETF feed rights for commercial display, repeatable peer-review workflow, precise costs and source coverage, ability to eliminate all factual errors, suitability of every published factor in a 12/24/36 month ETF context.

## Assessment of competition
Existing portfolio trackers, ETF screeners, macro dashboards, institutional research and analytical tools overlap pieces of the idea. Do not claim Aqil invented factor investing, economic regime models, causal forecasting or investment-memory systems. Potential distinction is **integrated research accountability + human-readable multi-depth global context + wealth discipline**. Market validation remains outstanding.
